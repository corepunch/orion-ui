# What small hotels complain about in their software

Desk research on what owners and front-desk staff of small hotels,
guesthouses, B&Bs and inns (about 5–40 rooms) dislike and want in hotel
property-management software (PMS), and what that means for Lodge. It
complements [small-hotel-needs.md](small-hotel-needs.md), which lists the
routine work Lodge must cover; this file records what goes wrong in the
products hoteliers use today.

## Method

- **Date:** searches run on 2026-11-21.
- **Tool:** a web search engine (standard mode, one extended query), about
  30 queries in English, German and Russian.
- **Queries:** product names plus "reviews", "cons", "support", "fees",
  "slow", "reports", "night audit", "rate plans", "walk-in", "keyboard",
  "offline", "data export", "contract"; Reddit-targeted queries
  (r/hotels, r/TalesFromTheFrontDesk); German (*Pension Hotelsoftware
  Erfahrungen*) and Russian (*мини-отель PMS отзывы недостатки*) queries.
- **Products covered:** Cloudbeds, Little Hotelier, Mews, Sirvoy, eviivo,
  ResNexus, Hotelogix, innRoad, WebRezPro, Oracle OPERA, plus smaller
  B&B tools that surfaced (Easy InnKeeping, ReservationKey, MyPMS,
  FrontDesk Anywhere, RMS, Hotelizer, eZee, protel, RoomKey, Bnovo).
- **Limits.** Page fetching was blocked in this session (Capterra and
  Software Advice did not resolve), so evidence comes from search-result
  extracts of review pages, not from reading each page. Quotes below are
  reviewer words as they appeared in those extracts; check them on the
  page before quoting further. Reddit threads did not surface in search
  (one TalesFromTheFrontDesk post via a mirror); German and Russian
  searches returned catalogues, not forum threads. Much of the
  industry writing (Cloudbeds, Mews, Stayntouch, WebRezPro, Hotelogix
  blogs) is vendor marketing and is marked as such. Review sites skew
  positive (most products average 4.0–4.8/5); the complaints are the
  minority signal, but they repeat across products.
- **Frequency labels:** *Very common* — appears for most products and in
  survey data; *Common* — several products or sources; *Occasional* — one
  or two sources.

## Complaints by theme

### 1. Too many clicks, cluttered or dated screens, long learning curve — very common

- A vendor-sponsored, PMS-agnostic survey of 500 hotel employees (Cloudbeds
  PMS UX Report) found the top pain points were too many clicks, too many
  manual tasks and poor integration. 52.2% of managers said staff need four
  months to three years to feel confident; 85% of staff get two weeks of
  training or less, mostly from a colleague.
- One manager in that report called the interface "confusing and
  overwhelming", needing several windows open for one task.
- OPERA: a G2 reviewer describes a dated UI with "so many screens that open
  over each other"; a Capterra front-desk agent says it demands too much
  attention to minute details, such as exact alignment when moving rooms on
  the room map.
- WebRezPro: "slightly dated", workflows could be streamlined "to reduce
  clicks"; "overwhelming… especially for staff who don't log in every day";
  multiple settings needed to change one function.
- Hotelogix: interface could be "less cluttered"; "learning curve is steep".
- Mews: "a highly circuitous system that overcomplicates basic tasks";
  weekly updates force staff to keep relearning.
- ResNexus: so many features "it can be overwhelming".
- Small B&B owners repeatedly say generic hotel software is too "big" for
  them (Easy InnKeeping reviewer).

### 2. Slow software, bugs, data that "disappears" — common

- Cloudbeds: long waits on every reload even when the status page shows no
  outage; "heavy and slow loading times"; Capterra's summary cites recurring
  bugs and bugs after major updates.
- innRoad: "very slow and you get the wheel of death"; "slow loading";
  data "disappears"; slowness "causes the system to be rebooted".
- These are cloud products; slowness is often a network round-trip on every
  screen.

Sources: [Cloudbeds on G2](https://www.g2.com/products/cloudbeds/reviews),
[Cloudbeds on Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/158839/Cloudbeds/reviews/),
[innRoad on Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/135307/innRoad/reviews/),
[innRoad on G2](https://www.g2.com/products/innroad/reviews).

### 3. Opaque pricing, add-on fees, per-booking commission, contracts — very common

- Cloudbeds: Capterra's summary lists confusing pricing, hidden fees and
  aggressive sales; one owner says subscription, add-ons and payment costs
  add up to a "significant monthly expense". Pricing is not public; G2
  estimates an average 19% negotiated discount.
- eviivo: monthly fee plus a per-booking fee; complaints of hidden fees,
  confusing invoicing, slow payouts, termination fees and being chased for
  fees after leaving; a B&B owner calls the fees "exorbitant".
- ResNexus: a 5-room B&B finds per-room pricing too high for its size; one
  owner left it as "Too expensive" for ReservationKey.
- Bnovo (Russia): channel manager, booking engine and passport scanning are
  paid add-ons.
- Contracts: auto-renewal windows of 90–180 days, early-termination fees on
  3–5 year terms (INNsight example: about $9,600 for a 40-room hotel), and
  a MyPMS reviewer warning about a 30-day cancellation notice.
- Sirvoy is praised specifically for transparent, low pricing.

### 4. Poor or unreachable support — very common

- Cloudbeds: "not possible to speak to support"; email support slow and
  unresponsive for urgent issues; G2 reviewer's email went unanswered.
- Mews: "the biggest issue is support"; human chat replaced by a chatbot;
  each reply opens a new email chain without context.
- Little Hotelier: no phone number; slow ticket answers; "We basically had
  to figure out all by ourselves" on rates.
- Hotelogix: issues take days, especially at weekends. eviivo: long chat
  waits, no after-hours help (2018). innRoad: does not return calls.
- Why it hurts small properties: problems happen at 23:00 at the desk, and
  there is no IT person (Stayntouch notes night-audit failures found the
  next morning because support was unavailable).

Sources: [Cloudbeds on Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/158839/Cloudbeds/reviews/),

### 5. Rigid rate and restriction setup — common

- Easy InnKeeping: a weekend minimum stay needs "a separate seasonal rate for
  every weekend!".
- Cloudbeds: rates must derive from one base rate (± amount or %), which
  does not fit negotiated corporate or group rates.
- FrontDesk Anywhere: one rate per room type, no length-of-stay tiers.
- MyPMS: wants a 2-night booking allowed when only a 2-night gap is open,
  despite a 4-night minimum (the "orphan gap" problem).
- RMS: "Setting new rates confuses me." WebRezPro: overwhelming, repetitive
  settings. ReservationKey: only one alternate rate plan per booking.
- Sirvoy: wishes for packages combining room and extras.

Sources: [Easy InnKeeping on Software Advice](https://www.softwareadvice.com/ca/property/easy-innkeeping-profile/reviews/),
[Cloudbeds on G2](https://www.g2.com/products/cloudbeds/reviews?page=2),

### 6. Weak, rigid or hard-to-find reports and accounting hand-off — common

- Cloudbeds: report system "limited", some reports only on request, finding
  reports "not clear".
- Little Hotelier: financial reports need refining; reviewers want one
  central report list; manual re-entry of invoices into Xero.
- Sirvoy: few report exports, "book-keping not possible to use".
- innRoad: wants friendlier, tailorable reports; protel: reports "pretty
  standard" and hard to customise.
- Mews: wants better dashboard reporting.

Sources: [Cloudbeds on G2](https://www.g2.com/products/cloudbeds/reviews),

### 7. Invoices, registration cards and printing — common

- Little Hotelier: invoices "unnecessarily complicated"; guests say so too.
- Hotelizer: producing each invoice by hand is slow and error-prone; email
  templates cannot be adjusted before sending.
- Hotelogix: wants more invoice layouts; posted transactions cannot be
  removed or hidden. eZee: invoices too long for groups; wants one
  consolidated group invoice.
- RoomKey: hiding the rate on the registration card also hides totals on
  travel-agent confirmations.
- Cloudbeds is noted for bulk or single printing of registration cards.

Sources: [Little Hotelier on Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/144307/Little-Hotelier/reviews),
[Hotelizer on Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/251146/Hotelizer-PMS/reviews/),

### 8. Double bookings and channel sync — common (out of scope for Lodge)

- Sources (mostly channel-manager vendors) agree overbookings usually start
  from a small mismatch between PMS and an OTA, manual edits on the OTA
  extranet, mapping errors, time zones and unstable Internet; one-way sync
  leaves the overbooking risk in place.
- Small hotels cannot afford accidental overbooking and have no
  distribution specialist.
- Hotelogix: channel connection "takes ages to set up"; Little Hotelier:
  "pricing still needs extranets".

Sources: [Smartness](https://www.smartness.com/en/blog/channel-manager-sync-errors-overbooking),

### 9. Internet outages and downtime — common in industry writing

- Cloud-only PMS stops the desk when the line drops: "manual check-in via
  pre-printed lists, with queues" (Stayntouch, vendor). Door locks and card
  terminals may stop too.
- Vendors' own advice is paper: Cloudbeds tells hotels to record check-ins
  and payments by hand during maintenance; Stayntouch and Visual Matrix
  publish outage checklists.
- A TalesFromTheFrontDesk post: Internet down before the audit, "couldnt
  do ANYTHING on my system", a day behind.
- Hospitality Upgrade's recovery series: re-key everything since the last
  audit, then re-run audits.
- German comparison sites still list on-premise software for pensions
  precisely because it "works without Internet".

Sources: [Stayntouch outage tips](https://stayntouch.freshdesk.com/support/solutions/articles/24000081058-helpful-tips-to-prepare-for-when-stayntouch-pms-is-offline),

### 10. Data lock-in and painful migration — common in industry writing

- Contracts rarely specify export format, timing or cost; folio history
  "rarely transfers as live records"; guest profiles are exported late or
  lost; occupancy/ADR history forgotten; proprietary payment tokens lock
  hotels to a payment vendor.
- Mews onboarding: manual migration of prepaid deposits left to the hotel.
- Russian users moved from Shelter/Bnovo to Lite PMS because of an open API.

Sources: [Smart Order: data export](https://www.smartorder.ai/resources/blog/hotel-pms-data-export/),

### 11. Payments, deposits and refunds — common

- Legacy systems without integrated payments make staff re-key cards
  (Mews, vendor).
- ResNexus: processing credits is "convoluted". Little Hotelier Pay: refunds
  impossible when the balance is zero; conflicting refund deadlines (7 days
  after check-out vs 60 days) in its own help centre. eviivo: slow payouts.
- Hotel Tech Report notes a large share of Little Hotelier reviews concern
  collecting payments, storing cards and payment requests.
- Night-audit guides stress that cash never on a folio must be reconciled
  separately; one case of about $87,000 skimmed through an unmonitored
  drawer at a small hotel.

Sources: [Hospitality Net legacy pain points](https://www.hospitalitynet.org/opinion/4124721.html),

### 12. Night audit — occasional in reviews, prominent in guides

- innRoad users like that the audit need not be run daily, and ask to
  "Lock night audit periods!!".
- Guides for small hotels: owners often do the audit themselves; skipping
  it compounds errors; rolling the business date before reviewing
  exceptions (no-shows, unpaid, OTA mismatches) is a common mistake.

Sources: [innRoad on Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/135307/innRoad/reviews/),

### 13. Housekeeping coordination — common in guides, rare in reviews

- Typical failure: reception does not know a room is ready and sends a
  guest to an unclean room; staff phone housekeeping for status. Vendor fix:
  clean/dirty/inspected status and refusing check-in into a dirty room.

Sources: [Hotelogix blog](https://blog.hotelogix.com/5-major-guest-complaints-that-a-cloud-pms-can-resolve/),

### 14. Check-in and walk-in speed — common in industry writing

- Vendor documentation (Amadeus, OPERA, Infor) shows walk-ins as multi-screen
  workflows: profile search, rate, packages, registration card, check-in.
  Stayntouch (vendor) claims up to eight steps and 5–8 minutes per guest on
  legacy systems. A Hospitality Net article lists staff "typing" instead of
  greeting as a warning sign; queues form when a flight lands.

### 15. Guest communication — occasional

- Little Hotelier: booking/cancellation text notifications vanish and cannot
  be looked up later. Hotelizer: cannot edit a template before sending.
- Localisation of confirmations (VAT line, city tax, language) is cited as a
  requirement; machine translation should be disclosed to guests.

Sources: [Little Hotelier on Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/144307/Little-Hotelier/reviews),
[Hotelizer on Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/251146/Hotelizer-PMS/reviews/),

### 16. Keyboard use, groups, fit for non-hotel lodging — occasional

- Mews feedback forum asks for shortcuts, above all to the search bar,
  because the same pages are revisited all day. OPERA users praise its
  search and "numerous shortcut keys"; keyboard-driven FOSSE is fast once
  learned.
- Groups: Little Hotelier "not great" for larger group bookings; Lodgify too
  simple for multi-room bookings.
- Sirvoy: "very much classic hotel" oriented, little flexibility for other
  lodging types.
- Mobile: Hotelogix "can't book using a mobile phone"; Sirvoy "No app".

Sources: [Mews feedback: shortcuts](https://feedback.mews.com/forums/918232-commander-pms/suggestions/40132633-shortcuts),
[OPERA Cloud shortcuts](https://docs.oracle.com/en/industries/hospitality/opera-cloud/23.5/ocsuh/cc_using_keyboard_shortcuts.htm),
[FOSSE guide](https://www.arhfoundation.org/fosse-hotel-system-guide),

users want software in their language and with local legal forms.

## What users wish for

| Theme | Wished-for features (as stated or directly implied) |
|---|---|
| Ease of use | Fewer clicks per task; one screen per task instead of stacked windows; usable after a short training; nothing that changes every week |
| Speed | Pages that open instantly; no wait on every reload |
| Pricing | Transparent, flat price fitting a 5-room B&B; no per-booking fees; no auto-renew traps; free to leave |
| Support | A human, by phone, when it breaks; context kept across replies; fewer cases that need support at all |
| Rates | Weekend/seasonal minimum stays without one rate per weekend; independent (not derived) rates; length-of-stay prices; gap-filling short stays; packages of room plus extras |
| Reports | One central list of reports; tailorable reports; clean hand-off to accounting (Xero) |
| Invoices & printing | Simple guest invoice guests can read; layout choices; one consolidated group invoice; edit a message before sending; bulk registration cards |
| Payments | Deposit and balance always visible; simple refunds and credits; cash drawer reconciliation |
| Night audit | Need not be run daily by hand; lockable closed periods; exceptions reviewed before the day closes |
| Housekeeping | Live clean/dirty status at the desk; refuse check-in into a dirty room |
| Outages | Keep working when the Internet is down |
| Data | Complete export of guests, stays, folios and history; open API/format |
| Keyboard | Shortcut to search; shortcuts for repeated actions |
| Groups | Multi-room bookings with one organizer and invoice |

## Implication for Lodge

Lodge is an offline-first desktop front-desk app with a local XML database.
Several of the strongest complaints (slowness, outages, lock-in, fees) are
problems of cloud subscriptions that Lodge avoids by construction; they
should be kept true deliberately and stated plainly.

| Theme | Product decision for Lodge |
|---|---|
| Too many clicks / learning curve | Every daily task starts from **Today**: check-in and check-out in ≤ 2 actions (select row, press Check in). One window per task; no stacked dialogs deeper than one. Target: a new employee works a shift after 30 minutes. Keep the UI stable between releases |
| Cluttered screens | Show only what the shift needs; advanced settings out of the main path. Each setting configured in one place (no duplicate settings, as criticised in WebRezPro) |
| Slow software | Local database: every screen opens and every save completes without perceptible delay at 40 rooms × several years of history. Add a performance test with a 10-year dataset |
| Room overview | A **room calendar grid (tape chart)**: rooms × dates, drag or keyboard to move/extend a stay, with overlap refused by the same checks as the form. No pixel-exact alignment needed (OPERA complaint) |
| Walk-ins / check-in speed | **Walk-in in one dialog**: dates, guests, free room (cheapest first), name, payment → booked and checked in. Registration form printed from the same dialog |
| Rigid rates | Rates as small tables: base price per room type, season ranges, weekday/weekend price, minimum stay per season or weekday, with an option to accept a shorter stay that exactly fills a gap. Independent rates, not only derived ones. Packages = room + extras |
| Deposits & payments | **Deposit and balance shown on every reservation** row and form; payments, refunds and credits as plain lines with method; cash drawer count per shift with variance |
| Night audit | **Night audit as one checklist** on Today: no-shows, overstays, unpaid departures, rooms not ready, cash count; resolve or acknowledge each, then close the day; closed days locked (corrections as new lines). Optional, not forced daily |
| Housekeeping | Room status (dirty/clean/inspected/out of order) on Today and the grid; check-out sets dirty; check-in into a dirty room refused (Lodge already does this); printable housekeeping list per day |
| Weak reports | One **Reports** list with plain names (occupancy, revenue by night, by source, payments by method, city tax, guest list); every report exportable to CSV for the accountant |
| Invoices & printing | One-page guest bill a guest can read; group bill across rooms; registration cards printable one or all of tomorrow's arrivals; templates editable as files |
| Guest communication | Confirmation and pre-arrival texts from editable templates, shown for editing before export/send; every sent/exported message kept on the reservation history. Email sending is optional and must not block offline use |
| Support burden | Clear in-app explanations of refusals ("Room 4 is still dirty — mark it clean or choose another room"); a **plain-language assistant / help pane** answering "how do I…" from the local manual; no feature requires calling a vendor |
| Pricing & contracts | One-time or flat licence, no per-booking or per-room fee, no account; say so in the README |
| Internet outages | Fully functional offline by design; nothing on the desk depends on a network. Shared mode (`lodge --remote`) on the LAN must not need the Internet |
| Data lock-in | Data is the documented XML file the hotel owns; full CSV export of guests, reservations, folios, payments and history; documented import from CSV for migration from other PMS; automatic dated backups |
| Find things | A **Find anything box** (Ctrl+F / F3) over guest names, rooms, reservation numbers, phone and email, from any page |
| Keyboard use | Accelerators for every daily command (new reservation, check in, check out, payment, find, next/previous day on the grid); full tab order; works without a mouse |
| Groups | Multi-room booking with one organizer, one bill and group cancellation (Lodge has a partial version) |
| Multi-language | UI and printed documents (bill, registration form, confirmation) translated through the string catalogs; guest documents printed in the guest's language independently of the UI language; local legal forms per country (see [country-readiness.md](country-readiness.md)) |
| Double bookings / channel sync | **Out of scope**: no channel manager. Lodge prevents double booking internally on every write; document that OTA inventory must be closed in the extranet or a separate channel manager, and offer an iCal/CSV export of availability as a later, optional bridge |
