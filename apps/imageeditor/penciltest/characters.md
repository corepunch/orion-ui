# Pencil Test characters

Black ink on white. Closed shapes so the head, jacket, shirt, trousers, and shoes can be filled later. No loose cloth.

## Coyote

A full-body ink drawing of an original lanky coyote on a plain white background, in the graphic line of a 1940s theatrical cartoon. He is built from a long wedge snout, tall stiff ears, a narrow chest, and a short fitted jacket that ends at the hip and lies flat against the body. He wears a porkpie hat seated between the ears, brim touching the ear bases, a plain shirt, narrow cuffed trousers, and two-tone spectator shoes. Both shoes point the same way. Solid black closed outlines, with the ears, nose, and shoe toes filled black and white inside every other shape. Small eyes sit high under a heavy brow, one tooth showing. Nothing hangs past the body: no coattails, no flying lining, no loose tie.

To make another animal in the same drawing, keep the pose, clothes, and line, and change only the head and ears.

## Look back and dance

Square frame. The body keeps facing right. The acting is the head, the hands, and the knees.

The coyote stands in side view facing right, arms down, hat on, both shoes stacked with the black toe caps on the right. He hears something behind him. His head snaps back over his shoulder until the snout points left and his eyes land on us, eyebrows up. The chest still faces right. He then drops into a bent-knee dance, one hand snapping up beside the shoulder and the other landing on his hip, still looking back. The short jacket stays flat at the hip. The hat stays on his head. Both shoes keep pointing right.
